Week of 12 October 2026

Advertising · Google

Google can now translate your ads into Dutch automatically

Google is testing a tool (in beta) that turns English search ads into versions for ten languages, Dutch among them. It translates headlines, descriptions and keywords, places them in a new campaign that starts paused, and can also translate the landing page. Advertisers can set rules such as "keep the brand name" or "use a formal tone".

Why it matters: Entering a new market gets much cheaper. But a correct translation is not automatically the right message for that culture, so human checks still matter for important ads.

Source: Slator, 12 October 2026

Social advertising · Meta

Meta lets advertisers run campaigns by chatting

At Advertising Week New York, Meta announced that its AI business assistant is testing "agent" features: advertisers type instructions to plan campaigns, create ads and change budgets or targeting. Meta also showed tools that make several versions of a video ad, and that turn product photos into product videos.

Why it matters: Day-to-day campaign work is shifting from clicking buttons to giving instructions. Meta claims 5% more conversions for advertisers using the assistant. Note that this is Meta's own figure.

Source: MediaPost, 7 October 2026

Research · Consumer trust

Marketers trust AI assistants more than consumers do

Kantar surveyed over 800 senior marketers and 23,000 consumers. A net 75% of marketers plan to spend more on visibility in AI assistants in 2027. Yet only 32% of consumers use AI assistants to research brands, and only 23% trust their recommendations.

Why it matters: There is a gap between where marketers are heading and where consumers are. Being visible in AI answers is useful, but trust still has to be earned elsewhere.

Source: Kantar, 2 October 2026
